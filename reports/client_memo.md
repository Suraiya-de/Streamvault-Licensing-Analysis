# StreamVault Content Licensing Recommendation

**To:** StreamVault Decision Team  
**Subject:** Evidence-based shortlist for the $14 million content licensing decision

## Executive recommendation

Prioritise franchise bundles for further licensing due diligence, while retaining selected standalone films as alternatives.

In the primary analysis, franchise bundles outperform standalone films on both weighted gross revenue-to-budget return and median unit return across the full historical sample and the post-2000 sample.

| Period | Licensing type | Weighted gross return | Median unit return |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| All years | Franchise bundles | 452.16% | 455.11% |
| All years | Standalone films | 384.95% | 284.48% |
| 2000 onward | Franchise bundles | 415.11% | 361.15% |
| 2000 onward | Standalone films | 324.04% | 284.48% |

These are historical box-office screening metrics, not licensing returns or forecasts of StreamVault's financial performance.

## Recommended franchise shortlist

The following candidates rank highest among the sampled post-2000 franchise bundles by gross revenue-to-budget return.

| Priority | Franchise | Films | Reported production budget | Reported gross revenue | Gross return |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | The Lord of the Rings | 3 | $266M | $2.92B | 996.45% |
| 2 | Ice Age | 3 | $229M | $1.93B | 743.21% |
| 3 | The Avengers | 4 | $1.111B | $7.92B | 612.81% |

**1. The Lord of the Rings — first priority for due diligence**

It has the strongest observed gross return among these post-2000 franchise candidates. Its three sampled films generated approximately $2.92 billion in reported gross revenue against $266 million in combined reported production budgets.

**2. Ice Age — second priority**

The three sampled films generated approximately $1.93 billion in reported gross revenue and a 743.21% gross return. Its reported aggregate production budget is lower than that of The Avengers.

**3. The Avengers — third priority**

The four sampled films generated the highest total gross revenue among these three candidates, approximately $7.92 billion. However, their combined reported production budget is also substantially higher, resulting in a lower gross return than the other two candidates.

Harry Potter is another franchise worth investigating. Its eight sampled post-2000 films generated approximately $7.76 billion in reported gross revenue and a 506.23% gross return.

These rankings indicate which candidates deserve investigation first. They do not establish rights availability, package composition or commercial affordability.

## Does the recommendation change after 2000?

The primary recommendation remains in favour of franchise bundles in both time periods.

However, the sensitivity analysis requires attention. When single-film collection candidates are reclassified as standalone units:

- **All years:** standalone weighted return rises to 458.57%, slightly above the franchise result of 452.16%. Franchise median return remains higher at 455.11%, compared with 400% for standalone units.
- **Post-2000:** franchises retain the advantage on weighted return (415.11% versus 328.84%) and median return (361.15% versus 325.21%).

This means the all-years weighted comparison is sensitive to the classification of single-film collection candidates. The post-2000 comparison provides more consistent support for the franchise direction.

## Talent evidence

Repeat appearances by directors and lead actors provide additional descriptive context.

Examples in the post-2000 sample include:

- Peter Jackson: six films and approximately 475.99% weighted gross return.
- Carlos Saldanha: two films and approximately 810.40% weighted gross return.
- Daniel Radcliffe: eight films and approximately 506.23% weighted gross return.
- Robert Downey Jr.: seven films and approximately 526.53% weighted gross return.

These are associations within the sampled films. They do not establish that individual directors or actors caused the financial outcomes. Shared franchise films can also result in identical aggregate figures for multiple contributors.

## Commercial deal note: the $14 million decision

The current analysis cannot determine how much StreamVault should pay for any of these titles or bundles.

The reported production budgets and theatrical revenues do not establish:

- Current licensing prices or rights availability.
- Available territories, licensing periods or exclusivity.
- Whether all films in a franchise can be licensed together.
- Expected viewing demand among StreamVault users.
- Subscriber acquisition, retention or incremental revenue.
- Contribution after licensing fees and relevant operating costs.

The $14 million should therefore not be allocated across these candidates using their historical gross returns. No defensible title-level spending ceiling can be calculated from the available data.

Before approving a deal, StreamVault should obtain actual quotations, confirm ownership and contractual restrictions, forecast expected audience demand, and calculate projected contribution after all relevant costs.

## Decision and next steps

1. Begin rights and commercial due diligence with The Lord of the Rings.
2. Evaluate Ice Age as the second franchise candidate.
3. Investigate The Avengers and Harry Potter as further alternatives.
4. Retain the standalone shortlist as a fallback if franchise rights or pricing are unsuitable.
5. Resolve the five unavailable candidate records and verify financial figures for priority titles.
6. Compare actual licensing quotations against forecast audience demand and expected contribution before committing funds.

**Conclusion:** Franchise bundles are the stronger initial screening direction, particularly in the post-2000 comparison. The Lord of the Rings is the first candidate to investigate based on the observed financial metrics. However, the final licensing decision must depend on verified rights, actual deal terms and StreamVault-specific economics—not historical box-office ratios alone.
